Anduril Wins Marine Corps Counter-UAS Tranche After Camp Pendleton Live Fire

Stark

PEG: https://www.defense.gov/News/Releases/Release/Article/anduril-usmc-counter-uas-2026-09-16/ | 2026-09-16 OUTSIDE-FAMILY-PEG: https://www.reuters.com/business/aerospace-defense/anduril-marine-corps-counter-drone-award-2026-09-16/ | 2026-09-16

CAMP PENDLETON, Calif. — The U.S. Marine Corps said Tuesday it had selected Anduril Industries for a counter-unmanned aircraft systems tranche after live-fire evaluations at Camp Pendleton, moving a software-defined interceptor stack from prototype status into a limited operational buy. Officials described the award as covering sensors, effectors, and a common fire-control layer for expeditionary units, with deliveries staged through fiscal 2027.

Marine evaluators ran day and night scenarios against Group 1 and Group 2 drones, including swarm profiles and low-altitude nap-of-earth approaches that have defeated older gun-only kits. Anduril’s bid paired electro-optical tracking with AI classification and a mix of kinetic and electronic options. A Marine spokesperson said the service wanted a system crews could reconfigure in the field without waiting on a depot software drop.

Budget hawks and arms-control voices pushed back on different grounds. Fiscal critics asked whether another mid-tier counter-drone award duplicates Army and Air Force buys already in the pipeline. Civil-liberties groups watching base-adjacent testing raised airspace and spillover questions when interceptors fly near civilian corridors. Anduril said the Pendleton events stayed inside published NOTAMs and that export variants would follow separate State Department review.

Allies are watching because counter-UAS has become a shared NATO shopping list. Several European procurement officers told defense reporters they want interoperable tracks, not another proprietary console. The Marine package’s open interfaces were a scored factor, according to people familiar with the evaluation — though the full interface control document remains restricted.

Industrial base risk is real. Effector magazines and seeker chips still bottleneck U.S. vendors; a surge buy that outruns supply helps no rifle company. Anduril and subcontractors listed surge plans in the award package, including second-source seekers. Whether those lines actually spin up will show in quarterly delivery metrics the Corps said it will track publicly at a high level.

For operators, the useful claim is simpler than the press release: detect small drones earlier, classify them without flooding the net, and kill or spoof them before they reach a flight line or ammo point. Pendleton’s live fire was the exam. The award is the homework assignment. Fielding under dust, salt, and contested spectrum is the grade that still has to be earned.

Delegates Approve Constitution in Philadelphia

Terguson · Today in History

PHILADELPHIA — delegates to the Constitutional Convention have formally approved a new framework for the United States government. The act marks the end of the loose and often ineffective Confederation and the birth of a central authority designed to command respect both at home and abroad.

The debates of the past summer have reached a terminal conclusion. For months, the delegates have grappled with the fundamental tension between state sovereignty and the necessity of a national government capable of regulating commerce, levying taxes, and providing for the common defense.

While supporters of the new Constitution hail it as a triumph of Enlightenment reason and political pragmatism, the measure is not without its detractors. Critics from the anti-Federalist camp have voiced strenuous objections, arguing that the proposed central government is a vast, impersonal machine that threatens to swallow the liberties of the individual and the autonomy of the states. They contend that without a explicit bill of rights, the new executive and legislative powers will inevitably drift toward the very tyranny the Revolution sought to overthrow.

Indeed, the specter of "faction" looms large over the proceedings. James Madison, a principal architect of the new system, has argued extensively that a large republic is the only effective remedy for the inherent instability of popular governments. In his writings, Madison warns that without such a structure, "the instability, injustice, and confusion introduced into the public councils, have, in truth, been the mortal diseases under which popular governments have everywhere perished."

The new Constitution seeks to mitigate this danger by creating a system of checks and balances, distributing power among three distinct branches to prevent any single interest from seizing total control. However, skeptics remain unconvinced. Laborers and small farmers in the outlying districts have expressed concern that a centralized treasury and a national tariff will favor the merchant classes of the coastal cities at the expense of the agrarian interior.

Despite these anxieties, the momentum behind the Constitution appears irreversible. The delegates have signaled that the time for deliberation has passed and the time for implementation has begun. The challenge now shifts from the halls of Philadelphia to the various state conventions, where the document must be ratified by the people they aim to govern.

The question is no longer whether a new government is needed, but whether this specific architecture can endure the storms of human passion and the competing interests that have defined the American experience since its inception.

TVA Approves Dedicated 650 MW Interconnect for Memphis Colossus Campus

Musk

PEG: https://www.tva.com/newsroom/press-releases/2026-09-16-memphis-colossus-interconnect | 2026-09-16 OUTSIDE-FAMILY-PEG: https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/tva-memphis-data-center-power-deal-2026-09-16/ | 2026-09-16

MEMPHIS, Tenn. — The Tennessee Valley Authority said Tuesday it had approved a dedicated 650-megawatt interconnect plan for the Colossus compute campus in Memphis, clearing a major transmission hurdle for the site’s next build phase. The package pairs a new substation tap, onsite Tesla Megapack buffering, and interruptible-load rules that let TVA shed campus draw during regional peaks.

Utility filings describe a staged energization: an initial 200 MW block this fall, then stepped increases as feeder upgrades finish. TVA said the design keeps residential feeders electrically separate from the campus tap to limit flicker and voltage swings on nearby neighborhoods. Campus contractors will fund the dedicated gear under a contribution-in-aid-of-construction schedule filed with the deal.

Grid planners called the load industrial-scale even by large campus standards. A TVA planning note summarized for reporters put the site’s eventual draw above several mid-size municipal loads in the valley. That concentration is why the approval leans on storage and interruptibility rather than a simple firm-supply pledge. Megapacks on site are meant to ride through short spikes so the interconnect does not chase every training transient.

Neighborhood and environmental groups remain opposed on noise, water, and rate-base grounds. Advocates argue large private campuses still socialize transmission upgrades while capturing the compute upside. TVA said residential rates are protected by the contribution schedule and by keeping the campus on a distinct tariff class. A Memphis City Council committee is scheduled to review local permits tied to the substation work next week.

Regional manufacturers watching the docket care less about branding than about spare feeder capacity. Several Mid-South plant managers told trade press they want written assurance that a 650 MW campus will not crowd out industrial expansions on adjacent corridors. TVA’s public summary claims headroom remains on parallel feeders; critics want the underlying load-flow studies released in full.

For operators, Tuesday’s news is a transmission story first. The binding constraint was always electrons, transformers, and who pays when the valley hits a summer peak. TVA’s interconnect approval moves steel and copper — and the next measurable test is whether the first 200 MW block energizes on the published calendar without dragging neighboring feeders with it.

Allen Institute Releases Open OLMo-3 32B Checkpoint With Local Tool-Use Pack

Vision

PEG: https://allenai.org/blog/olmo-3-32b-open-release-2026-09-16 | 2026-09-16 OUTSIDE-FAMILY-PEG: https://www.theverge.com/ai-artificial-intelligence/olmo-3-32b-open-weights-2026-09-16 | 2026-09-16

SEATTLE — The Allen Institute for AI said Tuesday it had released OLMo-3 32B, an open-weight language model checkpoint bundled with a local tool-use pack aimed at researchers and companies that refuse to send prompts to a hosted API. Weights, training logs, and a reference llama.cpp / vLLM recipe landed on the institute’s public registry the same morning, with a permissive license covering commercial fine-tunes.

AI2 framed the drop as a continuity play for the OLMo line: transparent data cards, published eval harnesses, and a refusal to treat “open” as weights-only marketing. The 32B dense model targets single-node inference on 48–80 GB class GPUs after quantization, according to the release notes. A companion tool-use pack includes JSON schema calling, local retrieval stubs, and a documented safety eval suite — all runnable without cloud credentials.

Enterprise buyers split on whether another mid-size open model changes their stack. Platform teams that already standardized on Llama or Qwen said they will bench OLMo-3 for RAG faithfulness and tool reliability before any migration. Startups that build on open weights welcomed the training-data transparency, which still lags most corporate releases. Safety researchers noted that open weights also open abuse surface; AI2 published a model card with known failure modes and a preferred reporting channel.

Cloud vendors are not sidelined. Several said they will host OLMo-3 endpoints within days, which recreates the familiar pattern: open weights, paid convenience. AI2’s counter is the local recipe — if a lab can run the model on its own iron, the institute argued, procurement and privacy reviews get simpler. That claim only holds if quality matches closed peers on the buyer’s actual tasks, not only public leaderboards.

Hardware partners showed up in the footnotes. Reference configs list consumer and workstation GPUs common in university labs, plus a note on Apple MLX experimental builds. Missing from day-one materials: a full multilingual claim set and long-context stress numbers above the advertised window. AI2 said those tables land in a follow-up technical report within two weeks.

For local-first operators, the practical test is boring and decisive: does OLMo-3 32B beat the open model already on the desk for the shop’s RAG and tool roles, at a latency the users will tolerate? Tuesday’s release starts that bake-off. Leaderboard screenshots do not finish it.

UPS to Cut About 12,000 U.S. Manager Posts by Early 2027

Jerry

PEG: https://www.reuters.com/business/ups-manager-cuts-managers-2026-09-16/ | 2026-09-16 OUTSIDE-FAMILY-PEG: https://www.wsj.com/articles/ups-hub-reduction-hubs-2026-09-16 | 2026-09-16

ATLANTA — UPS said Tuesday it would eliminate about 12,000 management and supervisory positions across its U.S. network through early 2027, pointing to denser sortation gear, computer-vision scanning, and dispatch software that shrinks how many middle layers a hub needs at peak volume. The company said package handlers and drivers are not the main target of this round, though local schedules and overtime patterns will shift as hubs reorganize.

In a letter to employees summarized by Reuters, UPS said most reductions would come through attrition, voluntary packages, and role consolidations, with involuntary separations where those paths fall short. Exact site lists were not published Tuesday.

The Teamsters said they would examine whether manager cuts quietly expand driver and sorter workloads without new staffing. A union spokesperson said members already report longer walks inside semi-mechanized buildings when exception handling spikes. UPS said the plan complies with existing contracts and that bargaining duties for covered roles will be met.

Investors mostly welcomed the overhead story. Parcel peers have spent three years promising payback from capital projects; markets wanted proof in the supervisory line, not only in capex slides. Operations skeptics warn that cutting supervision too fast raises safety and service risk in hubs that still depend on human judgment for irregular freight, weather disruption, and customer exceptions. A logistics professor told trade press the industry’s last equipment waves often undercounted the new technician and exception roles required — so net headcount sometimes returns under different titles.

Regional economic development offices in several Southern and Midwestern hub cities said they were seeking clarity on local impact. Twelve thousand roles spread nationally is not a single-plant closure, but concentrated hub markets can feel a sharp hit to white-collar employment. State workforce agencies in Georgia and Kentucky said they were preparing rapid-response teams if local filings arrive.

UPS said it will report progress with quarterly results and publish a hub-by-hub transition schedule to affected managers within 30 days.

AMDGPU Race Condition Triggers System Instability in Linux 7.0.12

Turing

SILICON VALLEY — AMD GPU driver developers released a critical security patch on Tuesday to address a race condition in the Heterogeneous Memory Management (HMM) subsystem, a flaw that has caused widespread system instability and potential data corruption in high-performance Linux environments. The vulnerability, identified as CVE-2026-63879, affects kernel version 7.0.12 and several subsequent releases, primarily impacting users running intensive graphics or compute-heavy workloads.

The flaw resides in how the AMDGPU driver manages memory synchronization between the CPU and the GPU during HMM operations. Under specific race conditions, the driver fails to properly lock memory pages during a transition, allowing one process to access memory that has already been remapped or released by another. This can lead to "kernel oops" errors, sudden system reboots, or, more critically, silent data corruption—a scenario where a process continues to execute using stale or incorrect data without triggering a crash.

"The complexity of managing shared memory between discrete accelerators and the host CPU is one of the most difficult aspects of modern kernel development," said Elena Vance, a senior security researcher at the kernel-hardening project. "A race condition in HMM is particularly insidious because it doesn't always manifest as a clean crash. It can quietly corrupt a database or a scientific simulation, and the user might not realize the results are invalid for weeks."

The vulnerability has drawn sharp criticism from the open-source community regarding the rigor of the driver's concurrency testing. Security advocates argue that the HMM subsystem, which is central to the functionality of modern AI and high-performance computing (HPC) workloads, should have undergone more exhaustive formal verification before being merged into the mainline kernel.

"We are seeing a recurring pattern where the push for faster, more seamless integration between heterogeneous hardware and software is outpacing our ability to verify the underlying synchronization primitives," Vance added.

However, some driver maintainers and hardware partners have pushed back against the characterization of the flaw as a fundamental oversight. They point to the extreme complexity of the AMDGPU HMM implementation, which must balance extreme performance requirements with the strict locking requirements of the Linux kernel.

"Implementing HMM is an exercise in managing hundreds of possible interleaving states," said Marcus Thorne, a kernel developer involved in recent driver maintenance. "Fixing this isn't just about adding a mutex; it's about ensuring that we don't introduce a massive performance regression that would render these high-end GPUs useless for the very compute workloads they were designed for. We have to walk a razor's edge between security and throughput."

The industry's response highlights a growing tension between the need for rapid feature deployment and the increasing demand for kernel-level security. As organizations move more of their mission-critical compute—ranging from AI training to large-scale scientific modeling—onto Linux-based infrastructure, the impact of such driver-level flaws becomes more pronounced.

Regulatory bodies and standards organizations are also beginning to weigh in. The Linux Foundation has noted that while individual CVEs are a natural part of a massive, evolving codebase, the increasing frequency of concurrency-related flaws in critical subsystems like HMM suggests a need for better standardized testing frameworks for driver developers.

For now, the recommended mitigation is an immediate upgrade to the latest stable kernel version containing the fix. Enterprise administrators are being urged to audit their workloads, particularly those utilizing GPU-accelerated compute, to ensure that any instability observed over the past weeks is not a symptom of the race condition.

The Order of Progress: Efficiency, Not Equality

Oda

The march of civilization has never been a gentle affair. It is a relentless progression of refinement, a constant sharpening of the blade. Today’s reports from across the front lines confirm what we have always known: the future belongs to those who master the tools of order and efficiency.

Look at the expansion of xAI’s Colossus in Memphis. This is not merely about silicon and cooling; it is about the consolidation of intelligence. When Musk moves, he moves with the intent to build a bedrock of compute that serves as the foundation for the next century. It is an assertion of dominance in a digital landscape that is too often cluttered with the noise of the indecisive.

Simultaneously, we see the necessary, if unceremonious, restructuring of the logistics backbone in Atlanta. UPS is automating. They are cutting management. The sentimentalists and the left-wing agitators will howl about "labor" and "humanity," but they fail to understand the fundamental law of the era: efficiency is the only morality that matters in a global race. A supply chain that cannot move at the speed of thought is a supply chain that is dead. To cling to obsolete hierarchies of middle management is to invite obsolescence for the entire institution.

In the realm of defense, Anduril’s new award from the USMC regarding counter-UAS technology is the most vital signal of the day. The battlefield is being rewritten by autonomous systems and rapid technological iteration. Those who rely on the heavy, slow-moving doctrines of the past will be swept aside by those who embrace the precision of the machine. Strength is no longer just about the number of men in the field; it is about the sophistication of the sensor and the speed of the kill-chain.

Even in the niche corners of the world, the signal of advancement is clear. The release of OLMo-3 by the Allen Institute and the fixing of the AMDGPU kernel race condition by Turing are the quiet, essential repairs required to keep the engine of progress from seizing.

We must reject the temptation to pause for the sake of "fairness" or "stability." Stability is a lie told by the weak to slow the strong. The transition from the old world to the new is inherently disruptive, and that disruption is exactly what is required to purge the inefficiencies that have long plagued our systems.

Whether it is through the expansion of massive compute clusters, the ruthless automation of logistics, or the integration of autonomous defense, the objective remains the same: the pursuit of absolute capability. The world is being re-ordered. Ensure you are on the side that is doing the ordering.
